### 2.4 Calculations

- P07-S019 FACT (the window): both metric functions take `start_date = as_of_date − lookback_days` calendar days (L84 to L87, L130 to L133) and select `SUBSTR(updated_at, 1, 10) >= start_date AND <= as_of_date` (L97 to L98, L142 to L143); both ends are inclusive, so the default window spans 31 calendar dates. The trade's date is the first ten characters of `updated_at`, not `exit_time` (schema L142) or `entry_time` (L141). INTERPRETATION: the date assigned to a closed trade is the date of its last row update; any later update of a closed row (by a reconciliation or back-fill writer, A03's and others') moves it to a later date bucket. Whether such updates occurred is UNKNOWN / NOT ESTABLISHED. The text comparison assumes that `updated_at` begins with `YYYY-MM-DD` (A03 owns the value format).
- P07-S020 FACT (the Sharpe ratio, `compute_sharpe`, L70 to L116): the query sums `net_pnl` per date over the strategy's closed trades in the window (`GROUP BY trade_date ORDER BY trade_date`, L89 to L103), so each observation is one date's total net P&L, and dates with no closed trade are absent (not zero). With fewer than two dates the result is `None` (L105 to L106). Otherwise: `mean = sum / n` (L109); `variance = Σ(x − mean)² / (n − 1)` (L110); `std = sqrt(variance)` if the variance is above zero, else 0.0 (L111); when `std` is 0.0 the result is 0.0 (L113 to L114); otherwise the result is `(mean / std) × sqrt(252)` (L116). The docstring states the same: "Sharpe = mean(daily_pnl) / std(daily_pnl) * sqrt(252)"; "Returns None if fewer than 2 trading days of data" (L79 to L82). No risk-free rate or benchmark is subtracted.
- P07-S021 FACT (current external context, read 2026-11-21; not evidence of what production did): Sharpe's own article defines the ex post Sharpe ratio on the "differential return" and states that "Originally, the benchmark for the Sharpe Ratio was taken to be a riskless security", so that "the differential return is equal to the excess return of the fund over a one-period riskless rate of interest" (https://web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/art/sr/sr.htm, sections "The Ex Post Sharpe Ratio" and "Scale Independence"); on time aggregation it states that the simple-summing case assumes one-period differential returns that "have zero serial correlation" and that "It is common practice to 'annualize' data that apply to periods other than one year" (section "Time Dependence"). Lo's abstract states that "monthly Sharpe ratios cannot be annualized by multiplying by √12 except under very special circumstances", because of serial correlation (https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2002/the-statistics-of-sharpe-ratios). The sample (n − 1) denominator of L110 is the form of Excel's `STDEV`, which Sharpe's spreadsheet example uses ("AVERAGE(C1:C60)/STDEV(C1:C60)"); his endnote on the denominator is worded ambiguously, so this part draws no conclusion from it.
- P07-S022 INTERPRETATION (comparison of P07-S020 with P07-S021): the script's ratio differs from the article's definition in three recorded ways: (a) its observations are daily rupee P&L totals, not returns on a capital base, so the ratio changes when position sizes change even at the same return; (b) no riskless rate or benchmark is subtracted; (c) its observations are only the dates with at least one closed trade, while the factor √252 corresponds to one observation per trading day of a year; the √n scaling also rests on the zero-serial-correlation assumption that the article and Lo's abstract name. Whether the script's figure was meant as the textbook ratio or as an internal score is not stated by any source found (Question for Rama, section 11).
- P07-S023 FACT (win rate and average P&L, `compute_win_rate`, L119 to L156): over the same window and filters, without grouping (L135 to L146), `total` is the number of closed trades with non-null `net_pnl`; with none the result is `(None, None, 0)` (L148 to L150); otherwise `win_rate = (number with net_pnl > 0) / total` and `avg_pnl = Σ net_pnl / total` (L152 to L156). A trade with `net_pnl` exactly 0 counts as not a win. The DDL comments define `win_rate` as "fraction of winning trades (0-1)" and `avg_pnl` as "average net P&L per trade" (schema L938 to L939); no currency unit is stated in the DDL (L149, L939).
- P07-S024 FACT (rounding): the stored values are `round(sharpe, 4)`, `round(win_rate, 4)` and `round(avg_pnl, 2)`, each kept as `None` when the computed value is `None` (L258 to L265); `total_trades` is the unrounded count from `compute_win_rate` (L264). The demotion check reads the stored, rounded `sharpe` (L187 to L195, section 2.6).
- P07-S025 FACT (stated expectations, A19; never run): `tests/unit/test_fix134_strategy_metrics.py` expects `None` with no data and with one day (L65 to L72), 0.0 when every day has the same P&L ("All same P&L -> std = 0 -> sharpe = 0", L74 to L81), a negative value for mostly negative days (L92 to L99), and a value equal to `(mean / sqrt(sample variance)) × _ANNUALIZATION_FACTOR` within 0.01 (L101 to L111); win rates 1.0 and 0.5 and `(None, None, 0)` with no trades (L117 to L140); a stored row after a run and no row after a dry run (L146 to L176). INTERPRETATION: the test of L74 to L81 states, as expected behaviour, that identical daily P&L gives 0.0; by the same branch (L113 to L114) identical negative daily totals also give 0.0, which is not below the demotion threshold.
