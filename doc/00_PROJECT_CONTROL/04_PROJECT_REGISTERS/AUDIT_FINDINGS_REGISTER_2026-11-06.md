## How entries are recorded

- **Topic:** each topic has its own section, and every row of a section belongs to that topic.
- **Finding ID:** the AUDIT's own item ID, so that every row can be traced to its record (the A01 AUDIT's section 1.7 explains its IDs).
- **Component:** the main file of the code path; the AUDIT record lists every path, with its function or class and lines, at the study baseline `970aabf` (D-21).
- **Area and flow:** taken from the AUDIT record, shortened where marked "(…)".
- **Finding:** one plain-English line, as put to Rama in the treatment round; the AUDIT record states the finding in full.
- **Issue category:** the kinds of issue of Document 04, section 5.3, assigned by Claude when this register was created (INTERPRETATION: the AUDIT does not assign them).
- **Intended behaviour and its source; code path; actual behaviour and effective value; evidence:** one column each. Each cell gives the opening of that field in the AUDIT's own words, shortened at a clause boundary where marked "(…)", and the line of the AUDIT where the full field is recorded, under the same field name ("Intended behaviour and source", "Actual code path", "Actual behaviour and effective value", "Evidence"); where a field continues in a table, the table's lines are given too. Line numbers are those of `A01_AUDIT_2026-11-05.md` as last changed at commit `2b27af4` (SHA-256 7b5d33471bb6d837634830a4e68d4226a3ce49e6ae94f04e66c921a34af25dfc). Words such as "above", "below" or "the cited lines" refer to that AUDIT record, and line references written "L123" are to the legacy code, as in the AUDIT. Every AUDIT record cited has all four fields; the AUDIT record governs. The column "AUDIT record" names the record by its section and ID. One opening is not given word for word: C29's evidence begins with a `git grep` pattern whose vertical bar would break this table, so its two alternatives are written with "or"; the AUDIT line gives the exact command.
- **Classification:** the result (ISSUE, or UNKNOWN / NOT ESTABLISHED) and the classification labels that the AUDIT record uses; KNOWN DEFECT as listed in the AUDIT's section 1.8. The AUDIT record governs.
- **Severity or impact:** not established for any A01 finding (the AUDIT assigns none). The AUDIT's own qualifiers (minor, latent, low practical risk, documentation only, a design concern), and its statements that occurrence in production is UNKNOWN / NOT ESTABLISHED, are shown.
- **Raised with Rama:** the date, with the Open Issues Register entry (A01-nn) or the treatment round's part or group.
- **Agreed treatment (Phase 2 input), or OPEN:** input to the Phase 2 proposal only (Document 04, section 5.5). It is not a V2 design decision, it changes no legacy finding, and no legacy behaviour becomes a V2 requirement automatically. Each group's treatment is set out once, under "A01 treatment decisions", with Rama's choice and the qualification he adopted.
- **Status:** the labels of Document 05, section 3: AGREED, or OPEN QUESTION; "AGREED in part; OPEN QUESTION in part" names the open part and the Open Issues Register entry that tracks it. UNKNOWN / NOT ESTABLISHED items have no treatment. No entry is CLOSED.
- **Not entered:** MATCH items (they stay in the AUDIT, section 6.3); items that the AUDIT rejected (for A01, C21b) or handed off whole (C38b); items merged into another and counted there (AUDIT section 1.8; for example M-10 in C19); and the parts handed to other topics (AUDIT section 8), which their owning topics record.
- **Sensitive values:** none. Credentials, tokens and account identifiers are named by type only.

- **Group A — Start-up failures that end with the wrong exit code or message (10 findings).** Recommendation (Claude): "one written start-up failure contract: every known failure has its own check, message and outcome (none ends in the generic crash code 2); each case has a test; the restart and alert rule as you agreed in A01-02 and A01-04 (3 attempts in total, then stop, Telegram and email); every required credential is named in the configuration and checked together at start, without exposing its value." Rama's choice, in the words of ChatGPT's section 8: "B — retain generic crash outcome for unexpected failures; distinguish permanent from retryable failures." Qualification (ChatGPT's review, section 4), adopted as part of the decision: "RECOMMEND B: accept, except clarify that the generic crash outcome remains available for genuinely unexpected/unhandled failures. Every anticipated, identified failure should have an explicit check, message, outcome, alert and test. Keep the already agreed retry/stop/alert policy from A01-02 and A01-04. Distinguish retryable transient failures from permanent configuration or credential failures; do not repeatedly retry a permanent failure without purpose. Required credential names may be checked, but credential values must never be exposed." Status: AGREED (06-Nov-2026).
- **Group B — Start-up order, and cleaning up after a failed start (10 findings).** Recommendation (Claude): "every start-up step is guarded; any failure runs ONE clean-up path that stops what was started, closes resources and alerts; stop signals are handled from the first moment; “ready” and order flags are set only on success; an optional feature is either fully on or fully off." Qualification (ChatGPT's review, section 4), adopted as part of the decision: "RECOMMEND A, with implementation-neutral safeguards.
